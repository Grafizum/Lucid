Lucid — what to look at, before and after

Every change from this round, as it was on the left and as it is on your phone on the right.

Nothing here needs code review. Each row is a thing to do on the phone once, and the “look for” line says what would make it wrong. The board cards already in review are the two at the bottom: sentence stepping and the rules.

WHAT TO CHECK, IN ORDER

1
Light ↔ dark, four or five times

Look for: one page dissolving into the other, not a colour flash. Tap it twice quickly — the second flip should land, not be swallowed. If the lens is up it will switch dress instantly; only the page dissolves.

2
Gear → Add apps → add one app → open that app

Look for: the lens already there over it. Close the lens, leave the app, come back — it should come back. Take the app out, open it again — it should not.

3
Home → Recent reads → Open

Look for: the reads you just made, grouped by day. Tap a row to unfold the answer; long-press to copy the original. The list survives closing the app.

4
Translate something long → Read the whole sentence

Look for: no button at all on a short answer; on a cut one, the button leads to the full text at reading size, and the sheet scrolls when it still runs past the screen.

5
Sentence by sentence on a long page

Look for: a counter and ‹ › arrows over a page of two sentences or more; each tap answers that sentence in the card, and one sentence means no arrows at all.

6
Anything you expected and can't find

Reader region was dropped on purpose — there is no region-picking mode to look for. If something else is missing, that is the report.

1 · Light ↔ dark

changed

Before: the rebuilt page arrived already in its new colours — a cut. Now: the old page is photographed, the new one comes up under it, and both fade on an ease-in-out curve in 190 ms. Press the buttons to feel the difference; the two phones switch at the same moment. One honest exception: the lens, if it is up, is a separate window and takes the new colours at once — it does not ride the dissolve.

4 · A translation longer than the card

new

Before: a long answer was squeezed into the card, and the tail was simply unreadable. Now: the card cuts at the sentence it cannot finish, and only then offers Read the whole sentence, which opens the same words at reading size on their own sheet — scrolling when they still run long. A short answer never shows the button.

5 · Sentence by sentence

in review

Before: each arrow read the line the lens was over, so a long page meant re-picking every line. Now: one read collects the whole page, and the arrows walk its sentences — the card answers each one in place, and the counter says where you are. The page underneath is not re-marked past the first read; that is deliberate.
