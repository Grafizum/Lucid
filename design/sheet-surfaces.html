Settings · the picker sheet

The box at the bottom, held in glass.

Four ways to carry the picker's surface. All of them keep the paper's warmth and the card's hairline, and all of them are see-through enough that the card that opened them still reads behind the sheet — the depth comes from a long shadow and a lit top edge rather than from a flat, opaque fill.

AParchment glass
Light
Dark

The default. Paper held at three quarters over a 20 px frost, thinning to just over half at the sheet's edge — so the card behind is still legible through the bottom of the sheet, and the sheet still reads as the nearest surface.

BClay glass
Light
Dark

Tied to the pair. A wash of the accent sits inside the glass and the border takes the accent hairline, so the sheet visibly came out of the clay card. The tint stays in the fill: no text ever sits on it at full strength.

CDeep lift
Light
Dark

Most solid, most depth. Nearly paper at the top and only a little thinner at the bottom, with a 44 px shadow doing the work. Best legibility of the four; the sheet is clearly a surface, not a veil.

DFrosted shell
Light
Dark

Quietest. The shell's beige in the glass over a 28 px frost, and the sheet fades out along its own bottom edge so it dissolves into the page instead of ending on a line. The most see-through of the four at the bottom.

What each one costs

A, B and D need a real backdrop blur. Android 12+ has one (RenderEffect); older phones would get the same translucent fill without the frost, which is still close because the fill carries most of the look. C needs no blur at all and is the safe one on a slow phone.

Rules kept in all four

The sheet never floats free: it is inset from the screen's edge and keeps the card's radius family. Its top edge is lit, its bottom edge is not. No text sits on a tint that has not been measured against it — the accent stays in the fill and the rim, never under 11 sp copy.
