Four ways to show light, dark and neither.

Three words cannot be understood without being tried. Each study below replaces the words with a picture of the result — a page in the light that choice gives — and keeps the word underneath for reading out. 01 is what the build does now; 02 to 04 are the bolder options.

01 is in the build. The previews stay literal in both themes — a dark page must look dark even while the app is light.

Three choices side by side, each a drawn page with three lines of text on it: paper by day, charcoal at night, and System as the two halves of one page. The chosen one is outlined in clay, its word takes the accent, and its mark follows.

03 the split card

Day and night are the same card.

One wide surface split down the middle — paper on the left, charcoal on the right — with a knob that rests at left, centre or right. Choosing Light or Dark pulls the whole app toward one half; System leaves it balanced on the seam.

04 big rows

One row per answer, each with its own window.

Keeps the list shape the card has now, but every row carries a wide preview of that surface on the left and the mark plus the consequence on the right — "Follows your phone", "Always light", "Always dark". Most explanatory, and the easiest to read with a screen reader.

In the build

What idea 01 changed

The three text rows became three cells on one row, each with a ThemeSwatch: a drawn page with a 9 dp corner, a hairline, and three text bars — paper with dark bars, charcoal with cream, and System split down the middle with each half keeping its own ink, clipped at the seam. The marks are the half-filled disc for System, a hairline sun for Light and the crescent for Dark, all at 12 dp and tinted at runtime.

The chosen answer is said three ways that do not depend on each other: a clay outline on its page, the accent tint and rim on its cell, and the accent on its word and mark. The hint line under the card still spells the consequence out — "Light, wherever the phone goes" — and the cell's content description still announces the choice for anyone listening rather than looking.

The swatch colours are the one place in the app that ignores the theme: a preview of dark has to stay dark while the app is light, or it stops being a preview.
