Home & dark,
before we change them.

Seven things on the home screen, five in the dark theme, and three decisions that shape both. This is the list I would want answered before touching the build — every row is the current state, the question, and the directions I can see.

01 · Home screen

Seven questions
about the first screen.

The page fits on one screen, which is rare and worth protecting. Most of these are about what that screen is for: a launch pad, or a control panel.

H1

The mark in the header

NowA 30dp copy of the launcher icon sits beside the wordmark, with its own cream tile and rounded corners.

Should the header show the launcher tile, or a bare lens glyph drawn at the same optical size?

WhyThe launcher already owns the tile. A bare mark reads as the brand, and it stops competing with the gear at the other end of the same line.

H2

The air under the header

NowAbout 170dp separates the wordmark from the intro line, so the card begins a third of the way down the screen.

Tighten the top so the card lands in the first fold, or keep the pause?

WhyEverything already fits; the gap is the only element with no job. Pulling the card up shortens the journey to the slide without crowding the header.

H3

Where the target language lives

Now“Translate into” sits in Settings only. On the home screen there is no sign of the language the lens will answer in.

Put a small language pill beside the wordmark, as the welcome screen does, or keep language in Settings?

WhyThe target is the setting people change most. Behind the gear it makes the first screen a status page rather than a control.

H4

One card, two kinds of row

NowThree lens switches — Auto-translate, Engine, Offline only — and two page links — Offline models, Translation history — share one card, divided only by hairlines.

Split them into “The lens” and “Pages”, or keep one card?

WhySwitches change behaviour in place; the other two leave the page. Two groups say that before the tap.

H5

State pills and action pills

NowOn / Online / Off and Manage / Open are the same filled pill, same height, same weight.

Give the two page actions a quieter treatment — a ghost button or a chevron — and keep filled pills for state. Or leave them identical?

WhyRight now “Online” and “Manage” read as the same kind of control. One is a value you own; the other is a door.

H6

Start the lens: drag only

NowThe well at the foot is a slider: the knob travels across to start the lens. Tapping the track does nothing.

Should a tap anywhere on the well also start it, with the knob kept as the visible handle?

WhyDrag is a deliberate gesture and worth keeping. Refusing a tap on the same control is the part that surprises people.

H7

Two footers on one line

Now“v1.0.2” sits at the left and “Shipped with Grafizum.com” is centred on the same line.

Move the version into Settings, keep both, or drop both?

WhyA version string is a diagnostic, not a signature. One quiet line is enough at the foot of a home screen.

02 · Dark theme

At night, the same
five ideas.

Dark is not the light theme inverted, but it currently inherits most of its structure. These are the places where the charcoal needs its own decisions.

D1

The mark stays cream

NowThe icon tile keeps its cream fill at night. It is the brightest surface on the screen by a wide margin.

Give the tile a charcoal variant, keep cream as a deliberate pin, or use the bare glyph from H1?

WhyCream at 30dp reads as a light left on. If H1 lands this disappears; if not, the tile should follow the theme.

D2

Three surfaces, one step apart

NowCanvas, card and pills sit within a narrow band of near-black browns. The hierarchy is carried almost entirely by the hairline.

Deepen the canvas and lift the card a step, or keep the low-contrast stack?

WhyAt night the card should feel laid on the page, not cut into it. One more step does that without adding glow.

D3

The filled accent pill

Now“On” and “Online” take a clay-brown fill. They are the loudest shapes in the card, louder than the lens slide.

Keep the fill, or move state pills to a tinted hairline with clay text at night?

WhyThe fill emphasises twice — colour and shape. The hairline keeps the state legible and lets clay stay an accent rather than a block.

D4

The slide nearly disappears

NowThe track and knob sit within a few points of the canvas; the arrow is the only part that reads at a glance.

Lift the track one step and the knob two, or keep it this quiet?

WhyThe one control that starts the product should be findable by thumb, not by memory.

D5

Hairlines carry the structure

NowDividers sit at roughly 10% cream. On a near-black panel they fade when the screen dims.

Raise the hairline to about 14%, add a soft inner highlight to the card, or leave it?

WhyThe rules are the only thing separating rows at night. A little more presence costs nothing in loudness.

03 · Open questions

Three answers that
decide the rest.

These are not changes; they are the choices that make the changes above easy or contradictory. Answering them first saves a round of redesign.

Q1

What is the home screen for?

NowHome answers “is the lens ready?” and “what is it set to?” at once, with Settings one tap away.

Should it be a launch screen — lens state plus start — or a control panel that also holds language, engine and pages?

WhyThe answer decides H3, H4 and H7. If home is a launch screen, those rows move out instead of being redesigned.

Q2

Does night keep the clay?

NowClay is the only accent in both themes; at night it turns brown against brown.

Keep clay for state at night, or let the dark theme lean neutral with olive for “on”?

WhyClay is the brand in daylight. At night warmth is harder to read; olive would say “on” faster but give up the accent.

Q3

Permission state on home

NowOverlay and text access are explained in Settings and the tour. Home says nothing until a read fails.

Show a one-line “needs access” strip when either permission is missing, or keep permissions in Settings only?

WhyThe first run can end with a lens that cannot draw. One line on home would explain the silence before the app gets blamed.
